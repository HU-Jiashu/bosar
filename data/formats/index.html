    Supported input formats and the quirks found on real data¶

    One section per reader. "Quirk" means something the files say that is not true or not what the format documentation promises; Bosar reports each one (W-code) and never corrects it silently. Started in v0.1.0.

    GAMMA / StaMPS export written by SNAP (data/gamma_stamps.py, reader gamma)¶

    Layout: rslc/<date>.rslc(.par), diff0/<ref>_<sec>.diff(.par|.base), geo/<ref>.lat|.lon, geo/elevation_dem.rdc, dem/. Big-endian FCOMPLEX / REAL*4, read through np.memmap windows. Validated on the author's Indianapolis IW2 stack (29 dates, reference 20240821, 25925 × 12239).

    # Quirk What the file says What is true Bosar
    1 Nominal slant-range axis range_pixel_spacing 2.0 m; near_range_slc / far_range_slc = center_range_slc ∓ (N−1)/2 × 2 m (819528.6 / 871376.6 m) spacing c / (2 · rangeSamplingRate) = 2.329562 m; center_range_slc (845452.572 m) is the first-pixel range of the SAFE annotation to the millimetre W201 on the stack; data import --annotation <SAFE> overwrites spacing, near/mid/far range and stamps geometry.provenance = safe-annotation:<file> (I201)
    2 Earth radius under the sensor earth_radius_below_sensor 6358256.3 m 6369807.5 m from the annotation orbit (WGS-84 radius at the sub-satellite latitude); the par value is 11.5 km too small and shifts every incidence angle by ≈ 1.5° and B⊥ by ≈ 8 m same W201 / --annotation (both radii replaced, incidence taken from the geolocation grid)
    3 Copied reference header every <date>.rslc.par carries the reference scene's title, date (2024 8 21) and start/center/end_time; only the state vectors differ per scene the scene date is the file name W201 once per stack; acq_utc = file-name date at day precision (2024-09-02); with --annotation the SAFE startTime of every date that has a SAFE
    4 Half-seconds times start_time 42612.60 s, center_time 42618.89 s, end_time 42625.18 s (of day) the SAFE says 23:40:25 – 23:40:52 UTC = 85225 – 85252 s of day: the par values are exactly half not used at all (see 3); documented here, not fixed — one stack is not enough to code a rule
    5 Geo layer .par is a copy of the SLC par geo/<ref>.lat.par says image_format FCOMPLEX, range_samples/azimuth_lines the layer is REAL*4 the reader accepts width/nlines or range_samples/azimuth_lines and never trusts image_format for a geo layer; the file size check guards the dtype (E203)
    6 Malformed .base sidecars initial_baseline(TCN) of 20240821_20240809, _20241231, _20250524 hold 1e11 – 1e15 m garbage (the same three dates MintPy dropped) E205 from parse_base; W202 on the stack, the interferogram is kept, the pair has no baseline row until v0.2.0 recomputes B⊥ from orbits

    Baselines: B⊥ = B_C cos θ − B_N sin θ with θ the look angle at the scene-centre range (from the radii and the range axis — hence quirks 1–2 move B⊥: 223.8 m nominal vs 216.1 m in SNAP's own metadata for 20240821_20240704).

    MintPy's bperp of this stack is not a perpendicular baseline. The author's ifgramStack.h5 stores, per pair, the raw C (cross-track) component of the .base TCN vector (gamma_utils.read_baseline; 261.21 m for 20240821_20240704 where the projected B⊥ is 213.9 m). Bosar always stores the projected B⊥ above. answer 4: the MintPy exporter writes the projected value; a --mintpy-legacy-bperp flag will reproduce the raw C component when a result has to be compared with the author's existing MintPy runs.

    Bosar crop (data/crop.py, reader crop)¶

    crop.json + little-endian tiled GeoTIFFs written by bosar data crop. Carries the source sha256, the window, the shifted geometry and its provenance (so a crop made with --annotation keeps the corrected scalars). No quirks — it is our own format.

    Sentinel-1 SAFE (data/sentinel1_safe.py, reader safe)¶

    • The author's unzipped SAFEs are VV-only and have no annotation/calibration/ (W203); the VH files listed in the manifest are absent (warning, VV opens).
    • The 20240704 IW1 measurement TIFF is a 1-byte placeholder → E203 naming SAFE, swath, file, actual and expected bytes; --swaths IW2,IW3 opens the rest.
    • Times in the annotation are UTC with microseconds; the geolocation grid holds degrees (converted to radians on read).

    Burst quirks (the native TOPS chain; data/burst.py)¶

    • A burst's azimuthTime is the zero-Doppler time of its line 0 (sensingTime is the raw acquisition start); the bursts of one date share the azimuth grid, so the start differences are integer multiples of azimuthTimeInterval to ~1e-9 lines.
    • The valid region is per line: firstValidSample / lastValidSample arrays with -1 on the invalid lines at both ends; ISCE2's rectangle (the run of valid lines, the samples common to its first and last line) is what the merge and ESD use. Consecutive bursts overlap by about 100 lines; the merged grid cuts the overlap in the middle.
    • azimuthFmRate and dcEstimate are lists of polynomials in τ − t0 with τ = 2 r / c; each burst takes the entry nearest its mid time (ISCE2). azimuthSteeringRate is in deg/s.
    • The PRF lives in generalAnnotation/downlinkInformationList/downlinkInformation/prf (older annotations may lack it; the model keeps prf_hz=None) — azimuthTimeInterval, not the PRF, is the line spacing of the focused product.
    • The raw pool's zips store the measurement TIFFs deflate-compressed (1.4 GB → 0.9 GB), so random access through the zip is not possible: extract_safe_members inflates manifest, annotation and measurement of one sub-swath into <project>/cache/safe/<name>.SAFE/ once.
    • Orbit state vectors in the annotation are seconds of day; a product near midnight gets vectors of the previous / next day (unwrapped by ±86400 s against the product start).

    Sentinel-1D next to Sentinel-1A, and newer processor versions¶

    Track 121 (ascending) over Indianapolis, sub-swath IW2, as read from the products on disk:

    IPF version 003.80 004.03 004.03
    radar frequency [Hz] 5 405 000 454.33 same same
    range sampling rate [Hz] 64 345 238.13 same same
    azimuth time interval [s] 2.0555563e-3 same same
    azimuth steering rate [deg/s] 0.97986 same same
    processed range bandwidth [Hz] 48.3e6 same same
    lines x samples per burst 1505 x 25925 1504 x 25389 1504 x 25389
    slant range time of sample 0 [s] 5.6402524e-3 5.6406765e-3 5.6406747e-3
    • S1D is handled like S1A / S1C: the same annotation schema, the same relative-orbit track (121), precise orbits named S1D_OPER_AUX_POEORB_... (ASF mirror, CDSE), product names S1D_IW_SLC__.... Nothing in the reader or the burst model is platform specific; the platform is recorded per date (secondaries.<date>.mission in the stack record) so that cross-platform interferograms can be told apart.
    • The burst size is not a constant of the track. The 2026 products (IPF 004.03, S1A and S1D alike) have one line and 536 samples fewer per burst and start 63.5 m farther in slant range than the 2024 ones. The native chain resamples every secondary onto the reference's burst grid from the geometry, so the mix needs no special case; a reader that assumes one burst shape per stack would fail.
    • Azimuth timing of S1D against S1A. ESD on the two S1D dates coregistered to the S1A reference converged to +0.0455 and +0.0468 lines (94 microseconds, about 0.65 m along track), against 0.0001 ... 0.009 lines (absolute) for the 49 converged S1A dates of the same stack: a constant along-track timing offset between the platforms, removed by ESD. Without ESD (geometry only) a cross-platform interferogram would carry a burst-edge phase jump of about 2 pi * 0.046 * dt * 4.5 kHz = 2.7 rad.
    • Range: the cross-correlation check gives -0.063 and -0.048 px for the S1D dates, inside the scatter of the S1A dates (-0.19 ... +0.12 px).
    • S1D phases 7 days after S1A on this track in mid 2026 (S1A 2026-06-24, S1D 2026-07-13 = +19 days = 12 + 7): cross-platform pairs have temporal baselines of 7 + 12 k days.

    The shifts do not cluster by IPF version — within almost every version they span ±0.1 px. They are propagation delays (scripts/range_shift_vs_era5.py): the change of the tropospheric delay (ERA5 zenith total delay at the crop centre, 1 / cos θ) and of the ionospheric group delay (40.31 TEC / f², 1.38 cm per TECU at 5.405 GHz; slant TEC from the CODE rapid GIM at the pierce point) between the date and the reference:

    predictor (87 dates) r R² residual std
    troposphere only 0.36 0.13 0.064 px
    ionosphere only 0.70 0.49 0.049 px
    troposphere + ionosphere 0.87 0.76 0.034 px (7.8 cm)
    both with free coefficients — 0.77 0.033 px; coefficients 0.94 and 0.83

    The ionosphere dominates (±0.18 px: slant TEC differences of up to ±30 TECU between dates near the 2024–2025 solar maximum; the reference date has 46 TECU). The coregistration geometry has no atmosphere, so the envelope of a secondary sits where its group delay puts it; the range correction moves the envelope back and leaves the carrier phase alone (verified with the two versions of one date: 0.005 rad across the crop). The measured shift is thus also an independent, if coarse, estimate of the TEC difference of each date (0.03 px ≈ 5 TECU).

    v0.4.0: the implied ΔSTEC per date is a product. bosar aux range-shift <project> --stack <id> [--era5-dir] [--ionex-dir] --write (pipeline/auxdata/rangeshift.py, which supersedes the v0.3.0 script) computes per secondary date the ERA5 zenith delay at the crop centre on the date and on the reference (the two hourly files around the acquisition, no download), tropo_px = Δztd / cos θ / Δr, residual_px = measured − tropo, and implied_dstec_tecu = residual_px × Δr f₀² / (40.31 × 10¹⁶) (168.8 TECU per pixel at 2.33 m and 5.405 GHz); with IONEX maps on disk the GIM slant-TEC difference at the pierce point stands next to it (gim_dstec_tecu), and the summary carries the regressions of v0.3.0 plus implied_vs_gim. --write stores the values in every date's coreg.json (record.range_cc.delay, with the sign convention) and in the coregistration step's summary (range_shift_delay, range_shift_delay_fit); bosar sbas quality then prints the shift[px], dSTEC[TECU] and GIM[TECU] columns in its per-date table. Sign: a positive shift is a later echo (more delay on the date than on the reference); ΔSTEC = STEC(date) − STEC(reference).

    Thermal-noise annotation and IPF versions (data/noise.py)¶

    annotation/calibration/noise-<mission>-<swath>-<product>-<pol>-<start>-...xml, one per sub-swath and polarisation, named like the calibration XML; read from the zip without extraction. Values are noise power in DN^2 (the unit of |DN|^2), linear. Two layouts:

    IPF elements noise power
    2.90 and later (from March 2018) noiseRangeVectorList/noiseRangeVector: azimuthTime, line, pixel (list), noiseRangeLut (list); noiseAzimuthVectorList/noiseAzimuthVector: swath, firstAzimuthLine, firstRangeSample, lastAzimuthLine, lastRangeSample (inclusive), line (list), noiseAzimuthLut (list) range(line, sample) x azimuth(line) inside each block
    before 2.90 noiseVectorList/noiseVector: azimuthTime, line, pixel, noiseLut range only
    • The IPF version is in manifest.safe: <safe:software name="Sentinel-1 IPF" version="003.80"/> under the processing metadata object (the annotation XML does not carry it). The Indianapolis products are 003.80 (2024) and 004.03 (2026).
    • IW SLC (xarray-sentinel's S1B 2021-04-01 test product, IPF 003.31, IW1, 9 bursts of 1501 lines, 21632 samples): one range vector per burst, 542 pixels every 40 samples ending at 21631, plus one vector at the product stop time; one azimuth block over the whole sub-swath (lines 0-13508, samples 0-21631) with a value every 10 lines that follows the TOPS scalloping (about 1.16 at a burst edge).
    • Quirk: the range vectors' line is one burst early in TOPS SLC. The vector whose azimuthTime equals burst k's azimuthTime has line = first line of burst k-1 (-1501 for burst 0; the calibration vectors of the same product map time to line correctly). Bosar picks each burst's range vector by azimuth time, as SNAP does; interpolating by line would give every burst its successor's vector (a few percent of noise power, tenths of a dB near the noise floor).
    • The azimuth line list and the block bounds are image lines of the sub-swath (burst k starts at k x linesPerBurst), the same frame as the calibration LUT.
    • tests/synthetic/safe.py writes <noise/> placeholders with with_calibration=True: an unusable annotation (E205 when read; thermal_noise=auto then skips the subtraction).

    SNAP BEAM-DIMAP (data/beam_dimap.py, reader dimap) — synthetic fixtures only so far¶

    • Abstracted_Metadata/radar_frequency is in MHz; .img bands are big-endian ENVI.

    ISCE2 topsStack (data/isce_stack.py, reader isce) — synthetic fixtures only so far¶

    • los.rdr band 0 is the incidence angle in degrees, band 1 the azimuth angle; reference/IW*.xml is needed for the wavelength and the slant-range axis.
    • Bosar's native TOPS chain (s1_coregister) writes this layout itself, with the addition DATA_TYPE = CINT16 (int16 I/Q pairs, BIP; half the bytes of CFLOAT) when asked for; the reader returns complex64 either way.
    • The stripmap chain (stripmap_coregister) writes the same layout with reference/<MODE>.xml (FBS.xml, IMS.xml, ...; the reader takes any reference/*.xml, IW* first) carrying dopplerCentroid (the JSON of the slant-range Doppler polynomial, {"r0_m", "coeffs_hz"}; absent = zero-Doppler), azimuthPixelSize, prf, lookSide, mission; orbits/<date>.json (each date's state vectors, so slc_flatten and the baselines need no project orbit file) and a coreg.json next to every SLC with that date's own centroid, offsets and refinement.

    IONEX 1.0 global ionosphere maps (data/ionex.py) - synthetic fixtures only so far¶

    Read for the order-of-magnitude test of the split-spectrum ionosphere (docs/design/ionosphere.md section 4). Format description: Schaer, Gurtner & Feltens (1998), "IONEX: The IONosphere Map EXchange Format Version 1".

    • Fixed columns. Every header and map record carries its label in columns 61-80. Header records read: EPOCH OF FIRST MAP / EPOCH OF LAST MAP (6I6, UTC), INTERVAL (I6, s), # OF MAPS IN FILE, MAPPING FUNCTION (COSZ / QFAC / NONE), BASE RADIUS (F8.1, km), MAP DIMENSION (only 2 is supported: a single layer), HGT1 / HGT2 / DHGT, LAT1 / LAT2 / DLAT, LON1 / LON2 / DLON (2X,3F6.1; km, degrees), EXPONENT (I6, default -1). START OF AUX DATA ... END OF AUX DATA blocks (differential code biases) are skipped.
    • Maps. START OF TEC MAP (I6 index), EPOCH OF CURRENT MAP, then per latitude band a LAT/LON1/LON2/DLON/H record (2X,5F6.1) followed by the band's values, 16 per line (I5); END OF TEC MAP. RMS maps the same (START OF RMS MAP); height maps (3-D files) are skipped. Values are in 10**EXPONENT TECU - an EXPONENT record inside a map overrides the header's for that map; 9999 = no value (NaN). Latitudes usually run north to south (DLAT < 0); a global grid repeats -180 as +180.
    • End of day. CODE writes the last map of a daily file as hour 24 of that day (2024 12 3 24 0 0); the reader accepts hour 24. An acquisition after the last map is served by merging the next day's file (merge_maps).
    • Interpolation (IonexMaps.vtec_at): bilinear between the four nodes; between two maps linear in time with the maps rotated by 360 deg/day (the "rotated maps" of the format description: a point keeps its local solar time); NaN off the grid or next to a missing node.
    • Single layer. Shell height = HGT1 (450 km for CODE / IGS), radius = BASE RADIUS (6371 km); sin z' = R / (R + H) sin z, slant TEC = VTEC(pierce point) / cos z', the pierce point z - z' of earth-central angle from the ground point toward the sensor.
    • Files. CODE final GIMs at http://ftp.aiub.unibe.ch/CODE/YYYY/: COD0OPSFIN_YYYYDDD0000_01D_01H_GIM.INX.gz (25 hourly maps, 00:00-24:00 UTC) since GPS week 2238 (2022-11-27), CODGDDD0.YYI.Z before (code_gim_candidates). Plain and gzip files are read; Unix-compress .Z files are refused with a hint (decompress them first). The library never downloads; scripts/validate_iono_winter.py does, into its default data/aux/ionex (another folder only with --download).

    NISAR L1 RSLC (data/nisar.py, reader nisar) - real granules opened, a real pair through the stripmap chain¶

    One HDF5 granule -> one StripmapScene per polarisation and frequency sub-band (open_stripmap(path, polarisation="HH", frequency="A", band="L"); frequency defaults to auto - A when the granule holds it, else the frequency it holds; polarisation to the first of the frequency's listOfPolarizations; band to the band the file holds, L before S). Registered as nisar; the sniffer accepts an HDF5 file (signature at offset 0 or after a power-of-two user block of 512 B to 64 KiB) that holds /science/LSAR/RSLC/swaths (or SSAR).

    The real granules (provisional L1 RSLC, NISAR_L1_RSLC_PROVISIONAL_V1)¶

    granule (cycle, track, frame, date) mode / pol code content size
    cycle 33, track 40, frame 50, 2026-10-14 0005 / NASV frequency B alone: VV, 5 MHz, 13680 x 6393 0.38 GB
    cycle 23, track 58, frame 53, 2026-06-18 0505 / SHSV frequency A: HH, 5 MHz; frequency B: VV, 5 MHz; 41040 x 6381 each 2.19 GB
    cycle 24, track 58, frame 53, 2026-06-30 0505 / SHSV as above (the 12-day repeat of the same frame) 2.19 GB

    What the reader found in them:

    • L band, left-looking, descending (lookDirection = Left, orbitPassDirection = Descending). The track 58 / frame 53 granules hold 41040 lines x 6381 samples per frequency, at a slant-range spacing of 24.98 m (25 m range pixels; the derived range sampling rate c / (2 dr) is 6.0 MHz for the 5 MHz processed bandwidth).
    • A zero-Doppler grid with its own line rate: zeroDopplerTimeSpacing is 1/1520 s (0.658 ms) while nominalAcquisitionPRF is 1911.1 Hz - the RSLC lines are not the radar pulses. The reader takes the line interval from zeroDopplerTimeSpacing (azimuth_time_interval_s) and keeps the PRF as prf_hz.
    • The mode and polarisation codes of the file name, read from these two kinds of granule (not from a specification): two digits of processed bandwidth in MHz per frequency, A then B (0005: no frequency A, 5 MHz on B; 0505: 5 MHz on both), and two letters per frequency (NA: not acquired; SH / SV: single polarisation HH / VV).
    • The centre frequency belongs to the frequency group, not to the letter: 1221.5 MHz on frequency A and 1236.5 MHz on frequency B of the 0505 granules, 1221.5 MHz on the frequency B of the 0005 granule (24.98 m slant spacing, the same first slant range - so the stacks of frequency A and B coregistered with the same crop are cell-for-cell comparable, which the two-band ionosphere estimator of iono_split --source bands relies on). The wavelength is each scene's own (processedCenterFrequency of the frequency opened). The stripmap chain does not compare the scenes' wavelengths: pair granules of one mode (with frequency="auto", a granule that holds frequency A and one that holds frequency B alone open different sub-bands).
    • Orbit: orbitType = MOE (medium-precision) in all three.

    Calibration¶

    calibration_db = -10 log10(K) with K = ext_cal_fact (MPP 1381). The ESA convention for single-look complex ASAR products (Rosich & Meadows 2004, ENVI-CLVL-EOPG-TN-03-0010) is sigma0 = |z|^2 / K * (R / range_ref)^3 * sin(incidence); the per-pixel range and incidence terms are the caller's (extra["calibration"] carries K and range_ref). With it the mid-image |z|^2 of 20030307 (mean |z| 66) gives -10.8 dB and that of ERS-1 19960516 (mean |z| 57) gives -15.4 dB for the same lake-bed area; the 4.6 dB gap is close to the 4.1 dB of 1 / sin 23 deg of the ERS convention (reference incidence 23 deg, Laur et al. 2004), so the ERS constant may follow the ERS formula. Not verified on a target: an open point for the data report.

    • ERS-2 lost its gyroscopes in January 2001; scenes from 2001-01-01 on get W224 (the Doppler centroid may be ambiguities large; the annotation less reliable).     DORIS and PRC precise orbits (data/doris.py, pipeline/auxdata/esa_orbits.py) - real files read¶

    The readers were written against synthetic files (tests/synthetic/envisat.py, still the fixtures of the tests); the real files now come from ESA's public mirror and have been read for the ASAR and ERS pairs of the stripmap chain.

    Where the files come from. ESA's own orbit servers need an EO Sign In account and FTP over TLS; the SNAP auxdata mirror (step.esa.int) serves the same files without a login, one zip per year:

    • ENVISAT DORIS: https://step.esa.int/auxdata/orbits/Doris/vor/<YYYY>.zip (DOR_VOR_AX, 2002-2012). Inside: <YYYYMM>/DOR_VOR_AX..._<start>_<stop>.zip, one inner zip per file; each file spans about 26.5 h (one state vector every 60 s) and is filed under the year and month of its stop date, so a file starting on 30 or 31 December sits in the next year's archive.
    • ERS PRC: https://step.esa.int/auxdata/orbits/ers_precise_orb/ERS<1|2>/<YYYY>.zip (1991-2011). Inside: <YYYY>/PRC_<yymmdd>_<orbit>_rev2.zip, one inner zip per file; each file is named by the day of its first state vector, spans four to eight days (one vector every 30 s) and is filed under the year of its start, so the last file of a year runs into January.

    bosar fetch orbits <project> --mission envisat|ers --product <IMS product> (or --date YYYYMMDD, with --satellite ERS1|ERS2 for ERS) downloads the year archives once into <project>/aux/orbits/esa/<mission>/ (the neighbouring year's archive too for a date next to the year boundary; a short or corrupt download is not cached, a cached archive that no longer opens is downloaded again), extracts the inner files that may cover each date into <project>/aux/orbits/esa/<mission>/files/, reads them back and keeps those that touch the date. A row is ok when one file covers a product's sensing window (the files together the whole day of a bare date), partial when files touch the day but leave a gap, missing when none does. That files folder is what the stripmap chain takes as --orbits <folder>.

    The formats.

    • DORIS DOR_VOR_AX... (precise) / DOR_POR_AX... (preliminary): Envisat format (MPH, SPH, one DS_TYPE=M data set named DORIS PRECISE ORBIT / DORIS PRELIMINARY ORBIT) of ASCII records dd-MMM-yyyy hh:mm:ss.ffffff delta-UT1 abs-orbit X Y Z VX VY VZ quality, one state vector per record (60 s apart in the real files), read by whitespace splitting (tokens 0-1, 4-6, 7-9, 10) as ISCE2's DOR class does. Earth-fixed frame, UTC.
    • PRC (D-PAF / DLR, "DGM-E04"): 130-byte ASCII records. The real files open with a DSIDP record before the STATE record (DSIDP ERS1.ORB.PRC / DSIDP ERS2.ORB.PRC): the sniffer accepts a file that starts with either. STATE carries the arc's first / last epoch (0.1 days since J2000) and the TDT - UTC offset in ms at bytes 47-52; STTERR records carry the day (bytes 14-20, 0.1 days since J2000 noon, + 0.5 day), the TDT seconds of day in microseconds (20-31), x / y / z in mm (31-67) and vx / vy / vz in micrometres per second (67-100), the quality character at byte 127; STTINE (inertial) records are skipped. Columns and the J2000 / TDT bookkeeping reconstructed from ISCE2's Orbit/PRC.py; the real files bear the day convention (ISCE2's + 0.5) out - their vectors agree with the products' own to about 10 m at mid scene. .Z files are refused with a hint.
    • The satellite of every file is recorded (OrbitRecords.satellite: ENVISAT, ERS1, ERS2, or none when the file does not say): PRC from the DSIDP record, else from the COSPAR id of the state-vector records (9105001 ERS-1, 9502101 ERS-2); DORIS from the SPH descriptor (ORBITE POE_REST SAT ENV1 on the mirror's files). It matters for ERS: the ERS-1 and ERS-2 files of a year share one files folder and cover the same times in the tandem months.

    How a product takes its orbit.

    • open_asar(path, orbit_file=...) replaces the product's five vectors by the file's, windowed to the scene +- 60 s with four extra vectors on each side (orbit_source = "VOR:<name>" / "POR:..." / "PRC:..."); a file that does not cover the scene is E205.
    • open_asar(path, orbit_dir=...) - the stripmap chain's --orbits <folder> - takes the file of the folder that covers the scene with 60 s to spare: VOR before POR before PRC, the longer span first within a kind; files that name another satellite than the product's are left out (files that name none are kept); none covering the scene is E201, with the fetch orbits command as the hint.
    • The chosen file is checked against the product's own state vectors at mid scene: more than 100 m apart is a W223; more than 1 km is not this product's orbit - a named orbit_file is refused (E204), a folder's file is skipped for the next covering one (W223), and E204 follows when none is left.

    On the real pairs: the DORIS VOR files of the ASAR pair 2009-12-25 / 2010-03-05 are 3-7 cm from the products' own vectors at mid scene (the Envisat products carry DORIS-derived vectors); the PRC files of the ERS-1 / ERS-2 tandem pair 1996-05-16 / 05-17 are 9.6-9.7 m from them (9.1-9.2 m along track, the same on both dates), and B_perp moves from -141.54 m (product vectors) to -141.58 m. The other ERS satellite's PRC file of the tandem day covers the same times about 11 950 km away - the case the satellite match and the 1-km check exist for.

    ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 L1.1 (CEOS) (data/ceos_alos.py, the same reader)¶

The quad modes are given their single-pol bandwidths (assumption: no quad product seen).

    Records. Data set summary, platform position (28 ECR vectors 60 s apart, high precision), attitude (16384 B), radiometric (CF at 20/16, −83.0 dB) and the image descriptor have the ALOS-1 offsets; five facility records of 3-728 kB are skipped by length. The signal data record's prefix is 544 bytes (descriptor field prefix bytes): the ALOS-1 fields to byte 216 plus the microseconds of day (uint64 at 84) and the ALOS-2 frame number at 284; the samples (COMPLEX*8, two big-endian float32) follow at 544. _prefix_dtype(prefix_bytes) builds either layout from the descriptor; nothing is assumed from the mission.

    What differs from ALOS-1 and how the reader takes it:

    • Time. The microsecond stamps give the line times directly (stamp_unit = "us" in extra["line_time_fit"]); the millisecond field is the truncated microsecond value (ALOS-1's is rounded). On both Greenland scenes the stamps' own clock runs 8.8e-6 faster than the leader's PRF (1826.4173 against 1826.4012 Hz: the line steps are 547 / 548 µs where 1 / PRF is 547.53 µs; ±44 µs at the scene ends over 10 s) - W221 reports it; the leader's PRF is used (the per-line PRF field agrees with it to the millihertz) with the centre-weighted fit of the stamps, so the scene centre matches the leader's centre time to the microsecond and the ends are off by 0.08 lines, which the coregistration's refinement absorbs. The check runs only on scenes spanning ≥ 1 s (STAMP_CLOCK_MIN_SPAN_S): below that the microsecond rounding alone exceeds the tolerance.
    • Range. The leader's range gate delay (132.53 µs) is not the two-way delay to the first sample (757 926 m = 5055.8 µs): it is counted inside a pulse interval (547.5 µs) and does not even equal the delay modulo the interval (128.0 µs). The line headers' integer metres are the starting range (as ISCE2's MultiMode/ALOS2.py reads it); the leader's value is kept in extra["range_gate_leader_us"] and not checked. Spacing c / (2 × 34.93 MHz) = 4.2913 m; the two Greenland scenes share the PRF, the starting range and the width (the lines differ by one).
    • Doppler. The cross-track centroid polynomial (1478, 3 × F16.7) is in Hz per pixel as on ALOS-1 (−35.36 Hz, −1.1e-4 Hz/px on the first scene) and, unlike ALOS-1, the Doppler center frequency fields at 1734 / 1750 (f = a + b R[km]: −19.49 Hz, −0.02096 Hz/km) agree with it at the first sample to 0.01 Hz; the reader checks that (W222 beyond DOPPLER_CHECK_HZ = 5 Hz) and uses the polynomial.
    • Pass and side. Clock angle +90 = right as on ALOS-1; the product id's A / D must agree with the orbit's vertical velocity at mid scene (E204 otherwise); the first line's centre geolocation must lie on the clock angle's side of the track (E204, as on ALOS-1).
    • Calibration. σ⁰ [dB] = 10 log10 |DN|² + CF − 32.0 with the leader's CF = −83.0 dB, as JAXA's format document states for L1.1 (calibration_db = −115.0; −11.3 dB on the Greenland ice at 36° incidence).

    • Bandwidth. range_bandwidth_hz is the leader's chirp |rate| × pulse length (24.9 MHz on both Greenland FBS scenes: 693.97 GHz/s × 35.88 µs), not the mode's nominal 28 MHz: the leader's total processor bandwidth in range (1254, 24900 kHz) and bandwidth per look say the same, and the measured range spectrum of the SLC is flat to ±12.45 MHz (−3 dB width 24.89 MHz) and 32-35 dB down at ±13 MHz (ISCE2's MultiMode/ALOS2.py takes the chirp too). The chirp is taken when it is plausible: positive, not above the sampling rate and within ALOS2_BANDWIDTH_TOLERANCE = 25 % of the mode's nominal value; else the leader's total processor bandwidth when it is plausible, else the nominal value, each fallback with a W222. The total processor bandwidth cross-checks a plausible chirp: more than BANDWIDTH_CHECK_REL = 2 % apart is a W222, and when both are plausible the narrower of the two is used, min(chirp, total) - the processed range spectrum is no wider than either (range_bandwidth_source = total_bandwidth_range when the total is the narrower; an implausible total leaves the chirp). In short:

      chirp total (leader) used W222
      plausible blank, or within 2 % chirp no
      plausible plausible, > 2 % apart min(chirp, total) yes
      plausible implausible, > 2 % apart chirp yes
      implausible / blank plausible total yes
      implausible / blank implausible / blank the mode's nominal value yes

      extra keeps chirp_bandwidth_hz, total_bandwidth_range_leader_hz, nominal_bandwidth_hz and range_bandwidth_source (chirp / total_bandwidth_range / nominal); the warning is in extra["warnings"]. The value reaches coreg.json and so the ionosphere step's sub-bands and the coregistration's interpolation kernel. oversample_to_fbs does nothing on an ALOS-2 scene (its FBD shares the FBS sampling). The ionosphere step's nominal-bandwidth table (NOMINAL_RANGE_BANDWIDTH_HZ, a fallback for coreg.json records written without a bandwidth) carries the ALOS-2 stripmap modes under mission ALOS-2: the fine modes FBS / FBD / FBQ take the 24.9 MHz measured on the JAXA fine-mode product (FBD / FBQ assumed the same, no such product seen), the ultra-fine and high-sensitive modes their nominal 84 / 42 MHz.

    • Polarisation. PALSAR-2 stripmap products may be VV or VV + VH (single HH / HV / VH / VV, dual HH + HV / VV + VH): without pol the reader opens HH, else VV, else the first polarisation listed (default_polarisation, extra["polarisation_source"] = default or requested), so open_stripmap and the stripmap chain open a VV product as they open an HH one. The stripmap chain refuses a product list whose scenes do not share one polarisation (E101, the dates and polarisations named: an HH product next to a VV-only one); its pol parameter opens one named polarisation of every product (handed to the reader as polarisation=, which the CEOS reader takes for pol).

    • One product per listing. list_product groups the files of a zip or a folder by their name tail (scene id + product id, ALOS2150521570-170307-FBSR1.1__A); a per-scan image IMG-<pol>-<tail>-F<n> joins the LED-<tail> product (so a ScanSAR product is one product, refused as ScanSAR, not 1 + N products). Only a tail with a LED- and an IMG- file is a product: a GDAL sidecar (IMG-HH-<tail>.aux.xml, written when QGIS computes statistics) or a stray file of an incomplete product is not one and is left out. Exactly one product per listing is opened: a folder or zip holding two products is E101 (naming them), a member file (LED- / IMG- / VOL- / TRL-) opens its own product (see "One product at a time" in the ALOS-1 section, which shares the rule).
    • Mission check. The file names say which mission; the leader's mission id (ALOS2) must agree (E205). The scene is mission="ALOS-2", sensor="PALSAR-2", wavelength 0.2424525 m, so the L-band quality profile applies as for ALOS-1.

    ALOS-1 PALSAR L1.1 (CEOS) (data/ceos_alos.py, stripmap reader ceos_alos)¶

    ASF's L1.1 products: the zip ALPSRP<orbit 5><frame 4>-H1.1__A.zip (FBD: members VOL- / LED- / IMG-HH- / IMG-HV- / TRL-<id>-H1.1__A, a quick-look and summary.txt, all stored) or ALPSRP<id>-L1.1.zip (FBS: the same files inside a folder <id>-L1.1/ with the processor's workreport and logs, all deflated), or the extracted folder. Opened with open_stripmap(path) / open_alos(path, pol=None) (HH, else VV, else the first polarisation of the product; ALOS-2 shares the rule); open_alos_scenes gives every polarisation. Validated on seven Mexico City scenes (frame 370, ascending, 2007-2011).

    One product at a time (both missions). The product files are grouped by their name tail (scene id + product id: ALPSRP082730370-H1.1__A, ALOS2150521570-170307-FBSR1.1__A). ASF's H1.1 zips and JAXA's ALOS-2 zips are flat, so two of them unzipped into one folder put two products side by side: a member file (LED- / IMG- / VOL- / TRL-) opens its own product - the scene's path is then that file, which re-opens the same product -, the folder itself is E101 naming the products, and so is a zip holding two products. Before the review the folder's files were merged by kind (the last product's files under the first one's scene id: a silent wrong scene on ALOS-1, a misleading E205 on ALOS-2). extract_product puts every zip into a folder of its own (<dest>/<zip stem>/). Only a name tail with a LED- and at least one IMG- file counts as a product (second fix of the review): a GDAL .aux.xml sidecar or the stray image file of an incomplete product next to a product is left out instead of making the folder E101, and a folder with no such pair is E201 listing the files found. ALOS-2 ScanSAR per-scan images (-F<n>) join their LED- product (ALOS-2 section).

    Sample format, decided from the descriptor (never assumed): 32 bits per sample, 2 samples per group, 8 bytes per group, format COMPLEX*8 / C*8 → two big-endian IEEE float32 per complex sample (I, Q). A product declaring 16 bits / 4 bytes would be read as int16 pairs; anything else is E204. The amplitudes are of order 1e5 (|DN|² ≈ 6e10).

    Conventions and checks:

    • Time. Every line carries its UTC stamp as integer milliseconds of day (half a line of quantisation at 2160 Hz); sensing_start_s is the least-squares intercept of the stamps against line / PRF (residuals uniform in ±0.5 ms, std 0.289 ms on the real product, which says the stamps are rounded, not truncated - a truncation would bias the start by 0.5 ms, one line; both the floor and the round writer modes are tested). The leader's scene centre time must agree (W222 beyond 2 ms, E205 beyond 1 s; the real products agree within the millisecond). The epoch is the midnight of the first line's day.
    • PRF. The leader's Pulse Repetition Frequency in mHz with decimals (2159.8272138 Hz) is used; the per-line field is the same number rounded to the millihertz (1e-7 relative). A variation across the lines above 1e-6 is E205 (one scene has one PRF), below it W221. ALOS-1 changed its PRF between scenes (2159.8 Hz on all seven Mexico City scenes, 1900-2200 Hz in general): pairs of different PRFs are the chain's business.
    • Range. starting_range_m is the leader's range gate delay × c/2 (845 217.56 m); the per-line field is the same in integer metres (W222 beyond 1 m, E205 beyond 100 m; constant across the lines or E205). Spacing c / (2 fs): 9.3685 m (FBD, 16 MHz) and 4.6843 m (FBS, 32 MHz). The FBS frame 370 scenes have 9344 samples where the FBD ones have 4640 (9280 oversampled) and start 300 m nearer: the grids coincide only after coregistration.
    • Doppler. The leader's cross-track Doppler centroid polynomial is in Hz per range pixel (f = c0 + c1 p + c2 p², p the 0-based sample; the quadratic term is 0 and the linear one has four significant digits in the F16.7 field). Checked on the real product: the correlation Doppler estimator on 4096 × 256 blocks gives +89.0 / +87.7 / +86.8 Hz at near / mid / far range against the leader's +76.4 / +75.0 / +73.6 Hz - the slope −2.8 Hz across the swath is the per-pixel reading (per metre it would be −26 Hz); the 12 Hz offset is the scene's spectral centroid (antenna pattern × backscatter), not the processor's reference. The leader's Doppler center frequency fields at 1734 / 1750 are a different quantity (136.3 Hz and −0.0707 Hz/km) and are not used. The scene stores the polynomial in slant range (p = (r − r0) / Δr), unchanged by the FBD oversampling. The L1.1 image is not zero-Doppler: rdr2geo of the scene-centre line at zero Doppler lands 0.8-1.2 km from the prefix's centre geolocation on every scene, and geo2rdr of that geolocation lands −f_dc / f_rate = 0.11-0.17 s (250-370 lines) later and within 1-2 samples in range, i.e. a target on line l has its zero-Doppler time at t_l − f_dc(r) / f_rate (the FM rate is the leader's cross-track rate, −518.9 Hz/s at the first pixel).
    • Orbit. The platform position record: 28 ECR state vectors 60 s apart, metres and m/s, high precision designator, covering 27 min around the scene; rebased to the scene epoch and densified to 10 s by 8th-order Lagrange so that the chain's cubic Hermite stays below a millimetre (0.3 m at 60 s). pass_direction from the vertical velocity at mid scene (the leader's pass flag is blank; its time-direction field says ASCEND and is kept as a hint).
    • Look side. Clock angle +90 → right (ISCE2's reading), confirmed geometrically: the first line's centre-pixel geolocation lies on the right of the ground track (E204 otherwise).
    • Calibration. σ⁰ [dB] = 10 log10 |DN|² + CF − A with the leader's CF = −83.0 dB (products processed after 2009-04-09; Shimada et al. 2009, IEEE TGRS 47(12)) and the L1.1 conversion A = 32.0 dB: calibration_db = −115.0, CF and A in extra. With −83 dB alone the Mexico City scenes would read +25 dB; with −115 dB a 256 × 256 centre window reads −6.0 to −7.8 dB (HH, urban / agricultural), which is the published range.
    • Mode. From the sampling rate (32 MHz FBS, 16 MHz FBD; four polarisations at 16 MHz would be PLR); range_bandwidth_hz is the mode's nominal 28 / 14 MHz (range_bandwidth_source nominal): the chirp bandwidth |rate × pulse length| (28.0 / 14.0 MHz on the real leaders, in extra with the leader's total processor bandwidth in range) agrees with it. oversample_to_fbs=True serves an FBD scene on the 32 MHz grid by zero-padding the range spectrum of whole lines (core/range_oversample.py; original samples kept, the band centred, the Nyquist bin split; the last block of lines is cached so column tiles do not re-read): n_samples, range_pixel_spacing_m and range_sampling_rate_hz are the oversampled grid's, range_bandwidth_hz stays 14 MHz. On the real FBD scene the oversampled spectrum holds 1.4 % of the in-band power between 7 and 8 MHz (the chirp's roll-off) and 0.04 % above 8 MHz (the split Nyquist bin).
    • Reading. One seek per line, the prefix skipped, only the requested samples; a stored zip is read in place through the member's absolute offset (2 ms for a 256 × 256 window), the deflated FBS zips through zipfile's seekable stream, which inflates the member from its start for every window (1.5 s at the scene centre, 2.5 s to open): extract_product puts the four files into a cache folder once (the chain does this, as for SAFE zips), and extra["deflated_members"] names the members concerned.

    Quirks found on the real products (frame 370, 2007-2011, processor EOC-ALOS-DPS 1.00): the data set summary leaves the pass flag and the centre latitude / longitude blank (the reader takes the centre line's prefix geolocation); the FBS zips are packaged differently from the FBD ones (folder inside, deflated, extra files); the chirp phase coefficients and the distortion matrices are blank; the volume directory's record count and product id fields are blank.
